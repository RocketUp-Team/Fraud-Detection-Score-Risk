The core-field table defines the shared vocabulary between the scoring service
and the dashboard. Probability is kept distinct from the display score, while
risk band and decision represent policy interpretation rather than raw model
output. The scoring mode records whether the request used the complete contract
or the reduced demonstration path, and the SHAP field carries local explanation
evidence for analyst review.

\section{Endpoint summary}

\begingroup
\small
\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Core REST Endpoints}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l p{0.48\textwidth} X}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{Path} & \textbf{Response}\\
\midrule
GET & \path|/health| & Process health status\\
GET & \path|/meta| & Model \& schema metadata\\
POST & \path|/score| & Risk score response\\
GET & \path|/transactions| & Paginated transaction queue\\
GET & \path|/transactions/{id}| & Detailed transaction record\\
POST & \path|/transactions/{id}/review| & Analyst review decision update\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}
\endgroup

The endpoint table groups the API by the user's workflow. Health and metadata
endpoints establish service and model state, scoring creates a risk assessment,
transaction endpoints support queue inspection, and the review endpoint records
the human outcome. This mapping makes it possible to trace a dashboard action
back to a specific backend responsibility.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\rowcolors{2}{ReportGray!25}{white}
\caption{Canonical feature groups}
\label{tab:feature-groups}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{p{0.22\textwidth}X}
\toprule
\textbf{Group} & \textbf{Representative features}\\
\midrule
Amount & \texttt{TransactionAmt}, log/capped/decimal amount, card-mean ratios\\
Time & \texttt{TransactionDT}, day, week, hour, day-of-week, age, night flag\\
Missingness & Selected identity/device missing counts and ratios\\
Presence & Identity, device, email domain, distance, address flags\\
Behavior & \texttt{dist1}, \texttt{dist2}, selected \texttt{C1--C14}, selected \texttt{D1--D15}\\

\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The feature-group table explains how the canonical vector is organised. Amount
and time describe the immediate transaction, missingness and presence features
describe the availability of identity evidence, behaviour features capture
anonymised patterns, and categorical fields preserve selected discrete signals.
The groups are explanatory; the model still consumes the exact frozen feature
order defined by the data contract.

The acceptance table converts the system requirements into repeatable smoke
tests. A successful handoff, model startup, full or partial score, and review
update together cover the main path from stored artefact to analyst action.
These scenarios demonstrate functional integration, but they do not replace
load testing, security testing, drift monitoring, or production recovery tests.
